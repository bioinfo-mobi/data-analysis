Coefficients of the current coordinate axes

Each new coordinate is a weighted sum of centred Gene A, Gene B and Gene C measurements. At the PCA orientation, these coefficients are the PC loadings.

Positive and negative loadings indicate contributions in opposite directions. Reversing a PC’s sign reverses all its loadings and scores without changing the PCA.

Presenter guide — about five minutes
  1. The analysis panels and explanatory message start hidden. Ask students to predict the widest direction using only the 3D cloud. Click Reveal analysis to display the projections, variance bars and loading plots. You can hide them again at any point.
  2. Start in original coordinates. The three coordinate strips have similar spreads, and the variance is distributed approximately 36%, 36%, 28%. Ask: “Can we choose a direction with more variation than any of these axes?”
  3. Click Transform to PCA. Use the 3D animation selector to choose either rotating axes around fixed points, or rotating points within fixed axes. These are two views of the same change of coordinates, and preserve all full 3D distances. You can switch at any rotation angle; the projection strips, variance bars and loadings remain identical between modes. The right-hand strips show the coordinates along each new axis. Only the final axes are principal components.
  4. At the final orientation, PC1 captures 75%, PC2 20%, and PC3 5%. PC1 is the direction of greatest possible variance; PC2 captures the greatest remaining variance perpendicular to PC1. The PC scores are uncorrelated.
  5. Show the three pairwise scatter plots. The original measurements have nonzero correlations. At the PCA orientation every correlation is zero: the score covariance matrix is diagonal. This means no linear correlation, not necessarily statistical independence. These plots use the full scores before projection, since setting PC3 to zero makes correlations involving that constant coordinate undefined.
  6. Click Keep PC1 + PC2. The cloud flattens by setting PC3 scores to zero. Dashed segments show the discarded differences. The 2D plot is unchanged because it already omitted PC3. We retain 95% of the original total variance.

The data are synthetic. Three deterministic random columns are centred, made orthogonal, and assigned sample variances in the exact proportions 75:20:5; an orthogonal rotation creates the original measurements. No standardization is applied. The bars always describe the full, unflattened dataset in the current coordinate frame; projection does not renormalize their percentages. A rotation preserves total variance and full Euclidean distances. Dropping PC3 can shorten distances. PCA axis signs are arbitrary. Intermediate coordinate variances need not be ordered, and intermediate axes need not be uncorrelated.
